# BCN: useful help, a community people return to, and sustainable revenue

Decision brief, 21 October 2026. This extends the first BCN 2.0 strategy with the owner's instructions to welcome startups and owners of all kinds, use blue, keep private conversations, and create practical help for people building an idea. Recommendations and experiment targets below are hypotheses, not forecasts or existing commercial agreements.

## The commercial decision

Make the product promise **Real help for real business owners**, with three equally clear doors:

1. **I am running a business:** solve a current problem with a tool, guide or considered introduction.
2. **I have an idea:** turn a dream into a specific, achievable request for skills, advice or practical help.
3. **I can help:** contribute experience, a short review, a skill swap, a genuine discount or a clearly priced service.

People should get an answer, a useful output or a relevant conversation before seeing a commercial recommendation. An owner who never purchases anything should still have a complete, useful experience. BCN can welcome every business type while starting with a manageable supply of help in pricing, websites, design, bookkeeping, software, first-customer research and operations. Financial calculations retain explicit UK assumptions.

The product advantage to test is **a clearer ask and a better next step**, with less noise and transparent commercial intent. Do not advertise BCN as already the world's best platform or claim competitor communities are unsafe. The owner's experience of clutter informs our design; it is not independently verified evidence about another platform.

## What has been built in response

- Blue public design; journeys for people starting out, existing owners and people who want to help.
- `/start`: a short practical pathway, official sources and a browser-only six-question starting plan with a downloadable text copy.
- `/ideas`: **Build it together**, with search and stage, help-category and support-type filters.
- Verified-account requests with a specific idea, practical need, next milestone, location, public display name and explicit publication permission. No fundraising category. Maximum two open requests; thirty-day expiry.
- Requests and offers require moderation. Public pages display neither account emails nor private offers. No likes, follower counts, algorithmic feed, public pitch stream or paid ranking.
- Free, discounted, paid and skill-swap offers have separate labels, scope, timing and terms. Paid prices are whole pence; a discounted offer needs a higher genuine usual price for the same scope. A moderator must assess supporting evidence; a numeric check does not verify that a discount is genuine.
- A helper explicitly agrees to chat if chosen. The request owner chooses a reviewed offer; that action opens the existing two-person BCN messaging model and records consent. It does not make a purchase, collect payment or create BCN revenue.
- Owner-reported progress is recorded privately, separately from a match and separately from money received. No automatic testimonial publication.
- Free private chat for verified accounts, recipient acceptance for directory chat requests, a separate profile opt-in to receive requests, blocking/reporting and archive controls. Historical conversations remain in the same existing tables.
- Existing video infrastructure reused only for accepted private chats when explicitly enabled, securely configured and permitted by existing administrator controls. Calling is disabled by default pending real two-browser staging checks. Group hosting retains its previous permissions.
- `/admin/bcn-ideas`: moderation, reports, consent/status audit and aggregate request/offer states. Existing introductions and evidenced BCN revenue remain in `/admin/bcn-help`.

## How to get the first useful exchanges

Do not launch a large empty social network. Prepare a small, balanced cohort and make each exchange work.

**Suggested controlled pilot, not an announced promise:** recruit 12–20 helpers with a stated skill and realistic contribution, and 15–25 owners with one achievable request each. Start only as fast as the moderator can review and the helper pool can respond. These are test recruitment goals, not current member numbers. Obtain explicit permission for participation; do not import separate businesses' contacts or mass-message existing members.

Offer helpers a bounded contribution: one thirty-minute review a month, one piece of prototype feedback, a first-customer interview or one genuinely discounted fixed-scope service. Let them say when they have capacity. Explain that helping can lead to a useful relationship, but does not guarantee leads, visibility or sales. Never require free skilled labour to earn a badge or unlock a sales pitch.

Use three initial matching themes so a visitor can recognise a relevant request:

- **First offer / first customer:** test an offer, ask prospective customers good questions and prepare a simple outreach plan.
- **Pricing / quoting / costs:** work through a real example with the free tools, then ask for relevant experience.
- **First website / prototype:** review a clear brief or first attempt before anyone buys a large build.

A closed small request can be replaced with the next milestone. Avoid publishing vague lifetime commitments such as “build my whole business for free”. Encourage reciprocal help, but never make helping someone a condition of receiving essential support.

### Operational launch sequence

| Window after a safe production release | Work | Evidence required to expand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–14 | Assisted pilot; review every request and offer; personally check whether the scope is achievable; keep direct video off until validated | Which asks get relevant offers, time to first reviewed offer, reasons for rejection, participant feedback and operator minutes |
| Days 15–30 | Publish up to three permissioned case studies; demonstrate one tool with a real worked example each week; invite a second small cohort through willing partners | A repeatable helpful exchange; no persistent unreviewed backlog; consent to every public story |
| Days 31–60 | Repeat the best two acquisition routes; test one service-introduction category with signed provider terms; consider one clearly scoped optional template pack | Actual qualified introductions and receipts; maintained help quality; no misleading or unresolved repeated offers |
| Days 61–90 | Expand only the topics where helpers respond; consider a sponsor-backed clinic or tool if there is audience evidence | Repeat participation, owner-reported progress, net revenue after operator time, complaints and helper retention |

No automatic email campaign, public launch announcement, spend, affiliate application or chargeable account was initiated. Outreach is a separate owner-approved operational action.

A public claim of “people helped” should state whether it means a conversation, a self-reported completed step or an independently corroborated result.

## Revenue that fits the promise

### First: introductions for people who explicitly want a service

Keep the private `/find-help` workflow separate from the peer-help board. An owner asking for a website supplier or bookkeeper can choose a named provider after receiving the commercial disclosure. An agreed provider fee is viable only if service value and collected margin justify the moderation and matching time. Use a written arrangement covering fee trigger, cancellations/refunds, evidence, complaints, data handling and payment date. Do not sell the same enquiry to multiple providers or charge for speculative lead lists.

Initially, charge **no automatic board commission**. Volunteers and owners should not discover a hidden charge after helping one another. If an optional managed procurement service is later tested, price and opt-in terms must be clear before it starts and must not change ordinary peer help retrospectively.

### Second: relevant software referrals, after approval

Public programme existence is verified; **BCN is not claimed to be approved**. No tracking links or commission rates are fabricated.

| Programme | Appropriate problem | Condition before use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SumUp [8] | An owner has a genuine need to take in-person payments | Approval and territory/marketing terms; compare transaction and hardware costs |
| Shopify [9] | A validated business needs a hosted ecommerce store | Active original website/audience and programme approval; do not push a store before an idea is tested |
| Hostinger [10] | A suitable self-managed website/hosting requirement | Approval; explain renewal pricing, support and limitations |
| Brevo [11] | Permission-based customer email/CRM need | Affiliate approval, use-case fit and accurate plan limits; distinguish agency and affiliate arrangements |
| Notion [12] | A workflow or project-planning need that suits the product | Current programme acceptance, territorial eligibility and contract terms; compare free alternatives |
| Xero [13] | An owner needs appropriate bookkeeping software | Verify the specific UK referral/affiliate route and eligibility; do not confuse accountant/practice partnership with ordinary affiliate approval |

A sensible first live test is **one** well-supported software decision journey, not six affiliate widgets. An original comparison should explain when to use a free option or buy nothing. Place a clear advertising/commission disclosure next to the recommendation and before engagement; a general footer disclosure alone is inadequate [14]. Count only verified commissions that are actually payable/received and reconcile reversals.

### Third: optional practical packs

Test demand through real requests before producing a catalogue. Strong candidates are a scope/change-control pack, a first-client onboarding pack or a job-cost-versus-actual tracker. A proposed £9–£29 test range is an experiment, not a current price or an available product. Keep quote editing/PDF, tool results, posting and basic help free. A useful pack must save setup time or add a structured workflow beyond the free page. Use sample pages, exact contents and clear terms before selling. Do not deploy checkout or take payments until product and tax/cancellation treatment are reviewed.

### Later: sponsor useful access, not trust

A sponsor could fund a practical clinic, a reviewed tool or an opt-in digest. Charge for a clearly labelled placement and a defined service, never for “trusted” status, favourable advice or moderation priority. Corporate sponsorship needs audience and delivery evidence. White-label calculators may become a separate B2B product once the tools are validated; do not let bespoke support distract from the core platform prematurely.

## Economics: the question is net contribution, not clicks

An illustration, **not a forecast or an agreed fee**:

- 5,000 relevant visits × 2% private help requests = 100 requests.
- 50% qualified × 40% consented introductions × 50% completed, payable engagements = 10 fees.
- An illustrative £75 collected fee = £750 gross receipts.
- At 20 minutes of operator time per incoming request, 100 requests use 33.3 hours. Valuing that time at an illustrative £20/hour uses about £667 before hosting, payment costs, complaints and tax.

This shows why buying traffic or accepting unlimited vague requests can lose money even when leads convert. These conversion rates, time assumptions and fees must be measured or negotiated; none is a benchmark or BCN result. Improve clarity and matching first. Do not make a high commission look profitable by treating the owner's time as free.

Track contribution as collected BCN receipts less refunds/reversals, direct fees, hosting/realtime/email costs and support/moderation effort. Provider turnover is never BCN revenue. Keep the separate businesses' accounts, customer information and revenue apart, with no visible promotion of the independent card business.

## What to measure

A weekly operational report should separate:

- Consented visits and completed free tools by landing page/source; untracked visitors remain outside those counts.
- New valid practical requests, moderation turnaround, first reviewed offer, chosen offer and owner-reported progress.
- Helpers who make another relevant contribution, unmet categories, expired requests and withdrawal reasons.
- Private help requests, qualified requests, named-provider consent, introductions and independently evidenced fee triggers/receipts.
- Affiliate clicks, verified commissions, received commissions and reversals as different stages.
- Report rates, repeat misuse, moderator minutes per request/offer and unresolved safety complaints.

The implementation records board states and audits as first-party operational data; it excludes private messages, individual idea paths and call pages from page analytics. Do not send briefs, offer text, quote figures or messages to analytics. Avoid A/B tests that promise response times or invent social proof. Choose an initial response-time commitment only after measuring actual capacity.

## Trust and operating requirements

- Assign a named moderator; inspect pending content and reports daily during the pilot. Pause promotion if the queue cannot be maintained. The board can close requests without deleting records.
- Check specific scope, truthful credentials, full prices and evidence behind discounts. ASA guidance expects usual/reference prices and savings claims to be genuine [15]; schema arithmetic cannot establish that evidence.
- Keep an accessible complaints contact for people who cannot sign in. Investigate unsafe conduct without automatically publishing accusations. Resolve reports with an audit note; record removals and appeals through the existing support process.
- Respect UK direct-marketing requirements. Sole traders and some partnerships are individual subscribers for electronic marketing rules; an account or a public email is not general marketing consent [16]. No bulk marketing was sent.
- Record an Online Safety Act scope assessment and, where applicable, illegal-content and children's-access assessments before a broad community launch. The adult-only terms checkbox is not proof that children cannot access a service. Ofcom explicitly covers user-to-user services and provides assessment/code guidance [17]. This brief is an engineering/operating preparation, not a completed legal compliance certification.
- Review data retention and implement an operator-led retention review before records reach their stated targets. No irreversible auto-deletion was introduced.
- Test real email verification/recovery, normal PostgreSQL, production limiter, consent/privacy settings and LiveKit/TURN on isolated staging. Video configuration readiness is not proof of real camera/microphone connectivity.
